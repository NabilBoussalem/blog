## Ingredients

- 6 large onions, thinly sliced
- 4 tablespoons butter
- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 1 teaspoon sugar
- 1 liter beef broth
- 250 ml dry white wine
- 2 tablespoons flour
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 bay leaf
- 2 sprigs fresh thyme
- Salt and pepper to taste
- 4 thick slices of rustic bread
- 200 g Gruyère cheese, grated

## Instructions

1. **Caramelize the onions.** Melt butter with olive oil in a large heavy-bottomed pot over medium-low heat. Add the sliced onions and a pinch of salt. Cook slowly, stirring occasionally, for 35–40 minutes until deep golden brown.

2. **Add sugar.** Sprinkle sugar over the onions during the last 10 minutes of caramelizing to help deepen the color.

3. **Add garlic and flour.** Stir in the garlic and cook for 1 minute. Sprinkle flour over the onions and stir for 2 minutes.

4. **Deglaze.** Pour in the white wine, scraping the bottom of the pot. Let it reduce by half.

5. **Add broth.** Pour in the beef broth, add the bay leaf and thyme. Bring to a simmer and cook for 15 minutes. Season with salt and pepper.

6. **Prepare the gratinée.** Preheat your broiler. Ladle the soup into oven-safe bowls. Place a slice of bread on top of each bowl and generously cover with grated Gruyère.

7. **Broil.** Place the bowls under the broiler for 3–5 minutes until the cheese is bubbly and golden brown.

8. **Serve immediately.** Be careful — the bowls will be very hot!

## Chef's Tips

- The key to great French onion soup is patience with the caramelization. Don't rush it — low and slow is the secret.
- Use a mix of onion varieties for more complex flavor.
- A splash of cognac added after deglazing adds a wonderful depth.
