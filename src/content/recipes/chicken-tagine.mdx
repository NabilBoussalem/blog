ingredients:
  - 8 bone-in chicken thighs
  - 2 tablespoons olive oil
  - 2 yellow onions, thinly sliced
  - 3 garlic cloves, finely grated
  - 1 teaspoon ground ginger
  - 1 teaspoon turmeric
  - 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  - 1/4 teaspoon saffron threads, bloomed in 2 tablespoons warm water
  - 1 preserved lemon, rind only, finely chopped
  - 140 grams green olives
  - 350 milliliters chicken stock
  - 1 tablespoon honey
  - 1 handful flat-leaf parsley
  - Sea salt and black pepper
instructions:
  - Season the chicken with salt and pepper, then brown it in olive oil until the skin turns golden.
  - Cook the onions slowly until soft and translucent, then stir in garlic, ginger, turmeric, cinnamon, and saffron water.
  - Return the chicken to the pot with preserved lemon, olives, stock, and honey.
  - Cover and braise gently until the chicken is tender and the sauce has reduced to a glossy broth.
  - Finish with chopped parsley and serve with couscous or torn flatbread.

## Why this recipe works

Chicken tagine depends on contrast. Sweet onions, sharp preserved lemon, warm spice, and briny olives all land in the same spoonful, but none of them should dominate. Browning the chicken first gives the finished broth a deeper base, which keeps the citrus from tasting one-dimensional.

<Tip>
Let the onions soften completely before you add the liquid. That extra patience turns the sauce from thin and sharp into something silky enough to spoon over couscous.
</Tip>

## Make-ahead notes

The dish tastes even better the next day. Cool it in its cooking liquid, chill overnight, then reheat gently with a splash of stock so the sauce loosens without breaking.

## Serving ideas

Serve the tagine with buttered couscous, torn khobz, or a simple shaved fennel salad. If you want brightness on the side, spoon over a little lemony yogurt just before serving.
